# Contributing Independent Projects

**Yantrika, Robotics & Coding Club, IACS Kolkata**

Share your own simulations and interactive models with the Yantrika community.
This page is the short, text version of the
[Independent Projects guide (PDF)](Yantrika_Independent_Projects_Guide.pdf).

> **Who is this for?** Students, alumni, hobbyists and first-timers from any
> school, college or university. You do not need to be a Yantrika member, and you
> do not need to belong to an institution: independent contributors are very
> welcome.

---

## 1. The big picture

Independent Projects is a showcase for work that students and curious people
build on their own. Submit a simulation, visualiser or interactive model related
to STEM, and once it has been reviewed it is listed on the Yantrika website.

Your project is **not** added to our code. It stays where you built it (your
repository, your website or your Drive) and the Yantrika site links to it and
credits you. You choose the tools, the language and the style, you keep your
copyright, and you choose your own licence.

| Principle | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| Open to everyone | Any student or user can submit, with or without an institution. |
| Your project, your home | We link to your work instead of copying it. |
| Any tool you like | Python, JavaScript, MATLAB, Julia, Scratch, a spreadsheet: if it simulates or models something well, it can be listed. |
| Reviewed before listing | A person looks at every submission first. |
| Credit where due | Your name and institution are shown next to your link. |

## 2. What counts as an independent project

We are looking for **simulations** and **interactive models** with a clear link
to a STEM subject. A visitor should be able to run it, play with it or step
through it and understand something a little better.

| Type | What it is | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Simulation | A program that imitates how a real system behaves over time. | Projectile motion with air drag, a random walk, heat flow in a rod. |
| Interactive model | A model with controls that shows how results change. | Ohm's law explorer, Fourier series builder, lens ray tracer. |
| Visualisation | A tool that turns data or a concept into a picture you can explore. | A graph theory visualiser, a phase portrait plotter, an orbit viewer. |
| Downloadable toolkit | Notebooks or files shared through Drive for others to run locally. | A Jupyter notebook series on numerical methods, with sample data. |

**Subjects we welcome:** physics, mathematics, chemistry, biology, computer
science, electronics, robotics, statistics, data science, earth and
environmental science, engineering and game theory.

**What does not fit:**

* Projects with no STEM link, such as pure entertainment or marketing pages.
* Anything that needs visitors to create an account, pay, or hand over personal details.
* Plain slide decks, essays or videos with no simulation or interactive part.
* Work you did not make and do not have the right to share.

## 3. How to submit

Submission is a single online form.

| Field | Required | What to enter |
|---|---|---|
| Full name | Yes | The name you want shown next to your project. |
| Affiliated institution | Yes | Your school, college, university or organisation. If you have none, write **Independent contributor**. |
| Project title | Yes | A short, clear name, ideally under 50 characters. |
| STEM area | Yes | The main subject, for example Physics or Computer Science. |
| Short description | Yes | Two or three sentences for the reviewers: what it simulates or models, and what a visitor can do with it. |
| Project link | Yes | One link of the kind described below. |
| Link type | Yes | Repository, hosted website, or Drive media. |
| Contact email | Yes | So a reviewer can reach you. It is never shown publicly. |
| Message for your listing | Optional | A few words about your project, shown under its title on the website. |
| Declaration | Yes | A tick-box confirming the project is your own work, or credited, and that you are happy for us to link to it. |

### Three ways to share your project

| Link type | What to share | What we check |
|---|---|---|
| Repository | A link to a public repository on GitHub, GitLab, Codeberg or similar. | There is a README that explains what the project is and how to run it. |
| Hosted website | A link to a page you host yourself, for example on GitHub Pages, Netlify, Vercel or your own domain. | It opens without a login, loads reliably and works on a phone-sized screen. |
| Drive media | A Drive (or similar) link to downloadable files: code, notebooks, executables, data and a short guide. | Sharing is set to "anyone with the link", files can be downloaded and a README is included. |

> **Not sure which to pick?** If your project runs in a browser, a hosted website
> is the easiest for visitors. If it is Python or another language that needs
> installing, share a repository. Use Drive for notebooks, large data or
> compiled files.

## 4. What happens after you submit

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1. Submitted | Your form arrives with the team. You do not need an account. |
| 2. Review | A reviewer opens your link, tries the project and checks it against the checklist in section 5. |
| 3. Feedback | If something needs fixing, a reviewer emails you with specific, friendly suggestions. This is normal and not a rejection. |
| 4. Listed | Once approved, your project is added to the Independent Projects page as a hyperlink with your name, institution and short message. |
| 5. Kept current | Links are checked from time to time. If a link stops working, we will contact you before removing it. |

### How your project appears

Each listing is a hyperlink to your project, shown with your name, your
institution and, if you wrote one, a short message about your project:

> **[Projectile Motion Lab](#)**
> by Your Name, Your Institution (or Independent contributor)
> Drag the sliders to see how launch angle and air drag change how far a ball flies.

Visitors click the title and go straight to your project. We do not host, copy
or edit your files.

### Add a message about your project

The form has an optional **Message for your listing** box. Use it to tell
visitors, in a few words, what your project is about and why it is fun or
useful.

| Do | Avoid |
|---|---|
| Keep it to one or two short sentences, ideally under 120 characters. | Long paragraphs, or repeating the project title. |
| Say what a visitor can **do**: explore, change, watch, run, compare. | Claims you cannot back up, such as "the best simulator ever". |
| Write in plain, friendly language a school student can follow. | Links, hashtags, ads, contact details or anything promotional. |

**Examples:** "Change the number of terms and watch a square wave appear." "A
random walker that shows why diffusion spreads out so slowly."

You can leave the box empty and your project will be listed with just its title,
name and institution. To change your message later, email us. Reviewers may trim
a message for length or fix small typos, and will check with you if anything
bigger needs to change.

## 5. Review checklist

Use this list to check your own work before you submit.

| Area | Questions the reviewer asks |
|---|---|
| It works | Does the link open? Can the project be run or used start to finish without errors? |
| It is STEM | Is it clearly a simulation or interactive model related to science, technology, engineering or maths? |
| It is accurate | Is the science or maths correct, and is any simplification explained? |
| It is clear | Is there a short description, instructions on how to use or run it, and a note on what it teaches? Is the listing message, if any, short, friendly and accurate? |
| It is safe | No malware, hidden downloads, tracking, ads, pop-ups or requests for personal data. Content is suitable for school students. |
| It is yours | Is it your own work, or are the sources and licences of third-party material credited? |
| It stays up | Is the link public and stable, not private, expiring or dependent on one login? |

## 6. Preparing each kind of link

> **Check before you send:** open your own link while signed out. If you can open
> and use it, so can a reviewer.

### A repository

* Make the repository **public** before you submit. Private links cannot be reviewed.
* Add a README with a title, a one-paragraph summary, a screenshot or GIF if possible, and run instructions.
* List what is needed: language version, libraries, and the exact commands.
* Add a licence file so others know what they may do with your code.

```bash
# a README should let a stranger run your project
git clone https://github.com/<you>/<project>
pip install -r requirements.txt
python main.py
```

### A hosted website

* Test the link in a private or incognito window to make sure no login is needed.
* Check it on a narrow window and on a phone. Buttons and sliders should be easy to tap.
* Show a short "how to use this" note and, if you can, the theory behind the model.
* Avoid popups, ads, cookie walls and trackers.

### Drive media

* Set sharing to **Anyone with the link can view** and make sure the files can be downloaded.
* Put everything in one folder, or one zip file, and add a README or a short PDF guide.
* Name files clearly and keep the size reasonable for people on slow connections.
* If a file needs special software, say which one and which version.

## 7. Rules every submission follows

1. **Original or credited.** Submit only what you made or have the right to share. Credit any third-party code, images or data.
2. **Educational and kind.** The project should teach or celebrate STEM and be suitable for school students.
3. **No harmful content.** No malware, scams, phishing, hidden downloads or anything that damages a visitor's device.
4. **No personal data collection.** No required sign-ins, hidden tracking or collection of visitors' details.
5. **Truthful details.** Your name, institution and description must be accurate. Independent contributors should say so.
6. **Keep the link alive.** Tell us if the link changes or the project moves.
7. **AI tools and licences.** If you used an AI tool, make sure its terms allow you to share the output, and say so in your README.

## 8. Licence, credit and ownership

* **You keep your copyright.** Yantrika only links to your project; we do not copy or relicense it.
* By submitting you give the club permission to **list your name, institution, project title, link and short message** on the website.
* You decide the licence of your own project (MIT, GPL, CC BY and so on). Please state it in your repository or README.
* Please do not use Yantrika or IACS logos in a way that suggests official endorsement of your project.

**Using someone else's material:** libraries, images, sounds, datasets and
snippets are fine if their licence allows reuse. Keep the required notices and
mention them in your README. Do not submit anything copied from a source whose
terms do not allow reuse. When in doubt, ask us or leave it out.

## 9. Tips for a great project

* **Start with one idea.** A small model that is explained well beats a large one that is confusing.
* **Let people play.** Sliders, buttons and instant feedback make a simulation memorable.
* **Explain the science.** A short note on the equations or assumptions turns a toy into a teaching tool.
* **Show what it does.** A screenshot, GIF or short walkthrough in your README helps reviewers and visitors.
* **Be honest about limits.** Say what your model ignores, for example friction or quantum effects.
* **Make it accessible.** Use readable colours and labels on controls, and do not rely on colour alone.
* **Test with a friend.** Ask someone who has not seen it to try it and tell you where they got stuck.

**Need an idea?** Choose something you studied this semester and make it move:
pendulums and oscillators, Fourier series, ray optics, heat diffusion, random
walks, cellular automata, graph algorithms, population dynamics, gas laws or
circuit behaviour.

## 10. Troubleshooting

| Problem | Likely cause and fix |
|---|---|
| The reviewer cannot open my link | The repository or folder is private, or Drive sharing is restricted. Set it to public or "anyone with the link" and test it signed out. |
| My website shows a blank page | Check the browser console (F12) for errors, and that file paths are correct and case-sensitive on hosts like GitHub Pages. |
| I do not belong to an institution | That is fine. Enter **Independent contributor** in the institution field. |
| I want to change my listing | Email us with the project title and what to update, such as a new link, message or corrected name. |
| I want my project removed | Email us and we will take it down from the listing. |
| I have not heard back | Reviews are done by volunteers, so please be patient. If it has been a long while, send a polite reminder by email. |
| I am stuck | Write to us. Beginners' questions are welcome. |
